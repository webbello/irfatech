Most of the businesses we talk to don't have a lead problem — they have a follow-up problem. Enquiries come in through the website, WhatsApp, and word of mouth, and a good number of them simply never get a second message.

## Where leads actually get lost

It's rarely one dramatic failure. It's usually a dozen small ones: an enquiry noted on paper that gets buried under the next day's work, a "I'll call them back" that doesn't happen because the person meant to make the call also runs the shop floor, or a lead that gets a first reply but no follow-up because nothing reminds anyone it's still open.

A CRM's job is unglamorous but specific: make sure every enquiry is visible, owned by someone, and followed up on a schedule — not on memory.

## What we build

For most SMBs, this doesn't need to be complicated. A working CRM setup covers:

- **A single place every lead lands**, regardless of whether it came from the website, WhatsApp, or a phone call logged manually.
- **Automatic follow-up reminders** — a lead that's gone quiet for three days surfaces itself, instead of relying on someone remembering.
- **A visible pipeline** — everyone can see what's open, what's stalled, and what's about to close, without asking around.
- **Reporting that doesn't require a spreadsheet** — where leads come from, how long they take to close, and who's actually converting them.

## Automation, not more headcount

The point isn't to replace a salesperson's judgement — it's to make sure their time goes to the conversations that need a human, not to remembering which of forty leads needs a nudge today. Automated follow-up sequences handle the routine "just checking in" messages; the person steps in when a lead actually replies or asks a real question.

This is usually where CRM work overlaps with WhatsApp automation: a lead that comes in through WhatsApp can be logged into the CRM automatically, and a scheduled follow-up can go back out the same channel, without anyone copying information between two separate systems.

## What it's connected to

A CRM that lives on its own, disconnected from your website and your ERP, is still better than a notebook — but it's not the full picture. Connected properly, a form submission on your website creates a lead automatically, and a closed sale updates your ERP without anyone re-entering the same customer details twice.
