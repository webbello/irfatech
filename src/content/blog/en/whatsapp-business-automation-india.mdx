For most of our clients, WhatsApp isn't one channel among several — it's where customers already are. Before we talk about automating anything else, we usually start here, because it's where the fastest, most visible improvement shows up first.

## Why WhatsApp first

A customer in India or Timor-Leste is far more likely to message a business on WhatsApp than to fill out a web form, wait on hold, or check email. That means every order update, appointment reminder, and routine question that currently goes through a phone call or a manually typed message is a candidate for automation — without asking customers to change how they already behave.

## Official Cloud API vs. unofficial tools

This is the part worth getting right before anything else: we build exclusively on the **official WhatsApp Business Platform (Cloud API)**, not the unofficial browser-automation tools that route messages through a personal WhatsApp Web session. The unofficial route is faster to set up and cheaper on paper, but it runs outside Meta's business messaging policies — accounts get banned without warning, and there's no real support path when it happens.

The Cloud API costs more to set up properly and takes longer to approve, but it's the only version we're willing to put a client's customer relationships behind. If a vendor offers you WhatsApp automation that doesn't mention the Cloud API, ask why.

## What to automate first

We generally recommend starting with the messages that are already predictable and already happening manually:

- **Order confirmations and status updates** — placed, packed, out for delivery, delivered — sent automatically as your ERP's status changes.
- **Appointment reminders** — for clinics, salons, and service businesses, a reminder sent a day ahead measurably cuts no-shows.
- **Routine replies** — hours, location, pricing tiers, and other questions that get asked daily but rarely need a human to answer them individually.
- **Payment and invoice reminders** — nudging without someone having to remember which customers are overdue.

What we deliberately don't automate on day one is anything that needs judgement — a complaint, a negotiation, an unusual request. Those route to a person, with the routine noise already cleared out of their inbox.

## Connecting it to the rest of your systems

WhatsApp automation is only as good as the data behind it. Connected to your ERP or CRM, an order update sends itself the moment status changes — no one has to remember to trigger it. Built in isolation, it's just one more system someone has to update by hand, which defeats the point.
